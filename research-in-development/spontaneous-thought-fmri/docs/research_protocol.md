# Research Protocol

## 1. Working title

**Sentence-Level Affective Content and Regional BOLD Activity During Verbalized Spontaneous Thought: A Proposed Secondary Analysis of OpenNeuro ds006067**

Short title: **Verbalized Thought Affect and Regional BOLD Activity**

Protocol status: proposed research plan; not preregistered; no empirical analysis has started.

## 2. Background and rationale

Spontaneous thought is a continuously changing stream of internally generated content. Its contents can vary in autobiographical relevance, semantic topic, sensory qualities, and affective tone. Affective features of self-generated thought are relevant to everyday cognition and mental health, and prior work has implicated regions including medial prefrontal, orbitofrontal, insular, and amygdala systems. At the same time, mapping unconstrained thought onto fMRI is difficult because the timing of thought formation is not directly observed and participants' speech introduces motor, auditory, and motion-related activity.

The OpenNeuro ds006067 dataset offers an opportunity to examine this problem with naturalistic think-aloud fMRI, sentence-level transcripts and timestamps, and sentence-level ratings of affective and sensory content. The proposed work is a secondary analysis of existing data, not a new data-collection study.

## 3. Overall aim

To test whether within-participant variation in the affective content of verbalized spontaneous thoughts is associated with regional BOLD activity beyond the modeled effects of speech production and prespecified nuisance factors.

## 4. Primary research question

**Within participants, is the dataset-defined sentence-level emotional-intensity rating associated with regional BOLD activity during verbalized spontaneous thought, after modeling sentence/speech events, head motion, and other prespecified nuisance factors?**

### Primary predictor

The dataset's `emotional intensity` rating, generated by GPT-5 Nano from sentence content and defined on a signed scale from −4 (very negative) to +4 (very positive), with neutral content near zero. The analysis will retain the original variable name in code and documentation. For interpretation, it will be described as a signed, valence-like annotation of verbalized content—not as an arousal measure, a direct participant self-report, or a physiological measure of felt emotion.

The score will be centered within participant (and scaled within participant if diagnostics support it) so that the primary estimate reflects within-person variation rather than differences in mean rating between participants.

### Primary neural outcome

The first-level GLM beta estimate for the affective parametric modulator, summarized within a small, independently defined ROI set. Candidate regions are medial prefrontal cortex, insula, and amygdala. Exact atlas labels and masks will be chosen and recorded before confirmatory outcome analysis, based on independent literature, image-space compatibility, and adequate voxel coverage.

## 5. Hypotheses

### Primary hypothesis (H1)

Within-participant fluctuations in the dataset-defined emotional-intensity rating will be associated with additional variation in BOLD signal in at least one independently defined affect-relevant ROI after accounting for the baseline sentence/speech regressor and prespecified nuisance regressors.

The primary test will be two-sided. The available literature motivates an affect-relevant ROI family but does not justify asserting a single directional effect for this exact dataset and rating operationalization. The primary ROI set will be frozen before inspecting primary outcome estimates.

### Secondary exploratory questions

- Are sentence-level sadness ratings associated with ROI-level BOLD responses?
- Are anxiety ratings associated with ROI-level BOLD responses?
- Are estimates similar in the subset of 18 transcripts with human ratings?
- Are results robust to alternative motion-control and HRF specifications?

Sadness and anxiety will remain exploratory because specific emotions are relatively sparse in the dataset and the dataset authors report that GPT-human agreement was weaker for anxiety (as well as joy and fear) than for most other dimensions.

## 6. Specific objectives

1. Audit the MRI, transcript, rating, and confound files and establish participant-level completeness.
2. Align sentence ratings and sentence timings with the fMRI acquisition timeline.
3. Quantify data quality, including mean framewise displacement, motion outliers, tSNR, missing annotations, and timing validity.
4. Estimate first-level affect-modulator effects using event-related GLMs.
5. Test group-level effects in a prespecified ROI family.
6. Evaluate robustness and annotation sensitivity without selecting the primary analysis based on observed results.
7. Publish a transparent, reproducible protocol and, after implementation, code and permitted aggregate outputs.

## 7. Design and sample

This is an observational, retrospective secondary analysis of a publicly available dataset. The data descriptor reports 118 healthy adults in the released MRI/transcript dataset. The included sample was aged 18–39 years and consisted of right-handed native English speakers recruited from a university community. Participants completed a 10-minute think-aloud session during fMRI. The dataset was acquired at 3T and uses a 1.5-second TR.

The final analytical sample will be determined from the actual files using prespecified inclusion and quality criteria. Published analyses of this dataset have used mean framewise displacement (FD) thresholds around 0.5 mm and have reported substantially smaller usable fMRI samples than the 118-person release. The project will not assume that all 118 participants are usable.

## 8. Proposed workflow

### Phase A — literature and dataset audit

- Read the dataset descriptor and all studies already using ds006067.
- Inspect the exact version and data-use conditions for OpenNeuro ds006067 v2.0.0 and OSF a56rm.
- Verify participant IDs across MRI, transcript, rating, and confound files.
- Inspect timing reference, missingness, transcript counts, rating distributions, and MRI derivatives.
- Record the provenance of each imaging derivative and decide which single signal stream is primary.

### Phase B — transcript feature preparation

- Join sentence-level transcript rows to the corresponding GPT-generated rating rows using verified participant and sentence identifiers or an explicit, validated text/timing join.
- Confirm there are no duplicate, unmatched, or shifted rows.
- Preserve original timestamps in seconds relative to the first functional volume.
- Center the emotional-intensity rating within each participant.
- Derive sentence duration, word count, and speech-rate descriptors where valid.
- Keep the human-rating subset separate and use it only for a sensitivity analysis.

### Phase C — fMRI quality control and timing

- Load the selected BOLD derivative and confound time series.
- Verify image dimensions, affine, TR, number of volumes, and acquisition timing.
- Calculate/inspect mean FD, framewise outliers, and tSNR where available.
- Verify that sentence event times fall within the usable acquisition window.
- Align event onsets and durations to the image time axis.
- Model the hemodynamic response by convolving event regressors with an HRF. Do not manually shift the event timestamps by 4.5 seconds and also convolve with an HRF; that could double-count the hemodynamic delay.

### Phase D — first-level analysis

For each participant, construct a design matrix containing:

- an unmodulated sentence/speech-event regressor;
- a sentence-level affective parametric modulator using the within-participant centered rating;
- motion and other justified nuisance regressors;
- speech-duration/production-related terms where they can be estimated without unacceptable collinearity;
- a thought-boundary or topic-transition term only if the corresponding annotations can be joined reliably and the design remains identifiable;
- appropriate drift/high-pass terms.

The primary contrast is the beta for the affective parametric modulator. The model should not use the same test data or observed outcome patterns to choose the HRF, ROI, or nuisance specification.

### Phase E — group-level analysis

- Extract subject-level affect-modulator estimates from each prespecified ROI.
- Estimate the group mean effect with a second-level random-effects model or equivalent subject-level inference.
- Report beta estimates, confidence intervals, standardized effect sizes, and corrected p-values.
- Use false-discovery-rate correction across the prespecified primary ROI family.
- Treat any whole-brain or network-level analysis as secondary/exploratory and apply appropriate whole-map or parcel-family correction.

### Phase F — robustness and reporting

- Repeat the primary analysis under a small, prespecified set of alternative motion criteria.
- Compare results using the fMRIPrep preprocessed BOLD image with explicitly modeled confounds versus the dataset-provided smoothed/denoised derivative, without regressing the same nuisance terms twice.
- Evaluate an alternative plausible HRF specification.
- Repeat the affective analysis in the 18-transcript human-rating subset, explicitly noting its reduced and non-representative sample.
- Report null and inconclusive outcomes as well as positive results.

## 9. Interpretation boundaries

This design tests associations between ratings of **verbalized thought content** and BOLD activity during/around verbalization. It cannot establish when a thought first emerged internally, whether the participant actually felt the emotion described, or whether the observed BOLD signal caused the thought content. It is not a clinical study and cannot support MDD diagnostic claims.

## 10. Expected outputs (planned, not yet produced)

- dataset inventory and QC report;
- transcript–rating alignment report;
- first-level design-matrix diagnostics;
- ROI-level subject estimates and group summary tables;
- sensitivity-analysis report;
- reproducible code and environment specification;
- final research report and, if warranted by evidence, a poster/manuscript draft.

No numerical result is assumed in this protocol.


## 11. Protocol status and scope

This document is a proposal-stage analysis plan. It has not been preregistered, and it is not evidence that data inspection, preprocessing, modeling, or statistical analyses have been completed. Changes made after feasibility checks will be recorded in the changelog with a reason and date.
