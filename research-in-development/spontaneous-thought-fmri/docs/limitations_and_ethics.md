# Limitations, Interpretation, and Data Governance

## Annotation is not subjective emotion

The primary values were generated by GPT-5 Nano from transcript content. They annotate the psychological content of verbalized sentences under the dataset's instructions; they are not direct reports of participants' subjective emotional states and are not physiological measurements of emotion. The signed −4 to +4 scale runs from very negative to very positive. The project will retain the dataset variable name, emotional intensity, while describing it analytically as a signed, valence-like content score.

## Human validation is limited

Four human raters evaluated 18 transcripts selected from transcripts below the median sentence count to reduce rater fatigue. This subset is not a representative human re-annotation of all participants. Human-rating analyses will be sensitivity checks only.

## Speech and fMRI confounding

Participants spoke aloud during fMRI. Speech production, articulation, auditory feedback, jaw/head movement, speech rate, and respiratory changes can influence BOLD signals. Motion and speech-related effects must be modeled or controlled where the available data permit, and the final usable sample must be reported.

## Thought-to-speech timing

The dataset includes timestamps for verbalized words and sentences, not a direct timestamp for the first emergence of the underlying thought. HRF modeling accounts for the delayed BOLD response to an event; it does not recover the exact time an internal thought began. Findings will be interpreted as associations between verbalized thought content and BOLD activity around verbalization.

## Observational design

This is a secondary analysis of observational data, not a controlled manipulation of emotional content. Associations do not establish causality and may reflect unmeasured cognitive, linguistic, behavioral, or physiological factors.

## Sample generalizability

The released sample consists of healthy, right-handed, native English-speaking adults recruited from a university community and aged approximately 18–39 years. Results cannot automatically generalize to clinical populations, other languages, other age groups, or naturalistic thought outside an MRI scanner. This is not an MDD patient cohort.

## Research novelty

Prior work has examined thought transitions, topic changes, semantic trajectories, and neural states, including analyses of ds006067. Separate work has modeled neural representations of valence and self-relevance in personal narratives. This project is positioned as a dataset-specific secondary analysis of sentence-level affective annotations, not as the first study of affective thought or valence-related neural activity. A fresh search is required before any novelty or publication claim.

## Clinical claims

The project will not claim to detect depression, identify clinical biomarkers, predict diagnosis, or validate a mental-health assessment tool. Questionnaire-based individual-difference analyses are outside the primary scope and would require a separate feasibility assessment.

## Privacy and data handling

Do not publish raw MRI, raw audio, full participant transcripts, questionnaire item-level responses, or potentially identifying content. Work with source data locally or in controlled storage. Publish code, documentation, and aggregate outputs only when permitted by source terms and applicable ethics/privacy constraints. Cite OpenNeuro, OSF, and the dataset descriptor.
